# Exercise Selection: Drew Baye Primary-Source Audit

This audit explains the source basis for the user's selected routine. Its comparisons do not independently change the prescription or constrain future user choices.

## Conclusion

The fixed 11-exercise workout implements Baye's basic machine template, adds stable knee flexion, and includes his direct neck extension/flexion recommendation. It deliberately omits the equipment-workaround dorsiflexion movement because the added setup complexity does not justify its marginal value.

In *High Intensity Workouts*, Baye says a minimum routine should include a squatting movement, vertical pull, vertical push, horizontal pull, horizontal push and trunk extension, then add direct abdominal, calf and neck work (PDF pp. 16–17; printed pp. 12–13). His example machine workout ends with calf raise, neck extension and neck flexion. The 2019 update supplies safe padded TSC dorsiflexion and says neck extension/flexion make lateral work unnecessary (PDF pp. 24–25). The 2025 supplement says his programs include the big six plus direct neck, forearms and/or grip, calves and shins, then identifies a leg-extension ankle pad and a 45° bench with yoga block as the practical equipment-limited implementations (PDF pp. 16–20; printed pp. 13–17).

This is a source-concordance audit, not an endorsement of Baye's one-set/failure/frequency doctrine. Dynamic sets, rest and cadence follow the broader evidence audit. TSC is used only where the gym lacks a dedicated dynamic machine; it is recorded without fictitious load or force.

## Full-spectrum check

| Region/function | Direct exercise or compound coverage |
|---|---|
| Quads and hip extensors | Leg press |
| Hamstrings/knee flexors | Hamstring curl; leg press and trunk extension contribute |
| Calves/plantarflexors | Calf press |
| Trunk extensors | Dual 600 Lower Back |
| Abdominals/obliques | Dual 600 Abdominals; compounds add bracing |
| Lats, teres, biceps and forearms | Pulldown plus row |
| Mid/rear shoulder, rhomboids and middle/lower traps | Mid-row plus pulldown |
| Chest, triceps and anterior shoulder | Chest press |
| Deltoids, triceps and upper chest | Overhead press |
| Neck extensors and flexors | 45° bench TSC neck extension and flexion |

The routine covers upper and lower body, all six basic movement functions, plantarflexion at the ankle, and direct neck flexion/extension. It does not directly isolate every anatomical subdivision. Baye himself says a huge variety is unnecessary and warns against multiplying curls, grips, and angles (PDF pp. 17–18; printed pp. 13–14).

Direct wrist flexion/extension and grip, hip flexion, hip adduction/abduction, and oblique flexion appear in Baye's expanded programs. They are not hard omissions here. Pulling already gives substantial forearm/grip work, leg press trains the major hip extensors and adductors, compounds require frontal-plane stabilization, and abdominal flexion plus bracing trains the trunk. Baye distributes these supplementary movements across rotating programs rather than requiring all of them in one fixed session. Adding them all would undermine the fixed, no-rotation routine for increasingly specialized returns; their exclusion is based on exercise value and duplication, not an artificial session deadline.

## Why these alternatives are outside the current routine

- DB stiff-leg/Romanian deadlift: the Dual 400 Hamstring Curl plus Dual 600 Lower Back cover the intended hamstring/trunk work with less balance and hinge-skill demand.
- DB bench press: duplicates the Dual 300 chest press.
- Lateral raise and shrug: overhead pressing, rowing and pulldown already train the shoulder girdle; Baye's own text rejects unnecessary variation.
- Curl: pulldown and row already train elbow flexors, and the goal is not maximal arm specialization.
- Standing DB calf raise: Baye gives the leg-press substitution explicitly.
- Floor abdominal drills and static holds: the Dual 600 Abdominals supply direct, loadable abdominal work on confirmed equipment.
- Leg extension: the leg press already supplies the knee-dominant function; the leg curl adds the missing knee-flexion function without redundant quadriceps isolation.
- Smith squat: duplicates the leg press and adds setup/coordination cost without filling a missing pattern.

## Exercise order

Baye recommends large compound work first, alternating pulling with non-pulling work, and placing direct trunk work after movements needing trunk stability (PDF pp. 50–51; printed pp. 46–47). He also allows order changes to prioritize an exercise. The recommended order in `prescription.strength.recommendedOrder` follows that logic but is a suggestion. Any order around availability is acceptable; the ledger stores no exercise-order or swap state, and progression depends only on each exercise's actual exposures.
