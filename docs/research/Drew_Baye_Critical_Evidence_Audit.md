# Drew Baye: Critical Evidence Audit

**Evidence current through August 26, 2026**  
**Primary-source layer:** [Drew_Baye_Book_Claims_Catalog.md](Drew_Baye_Book_Claims_Catalog.md)

The practical implications below are evidence analysis. The user's selected prescription is in `src/policy/prescription.ts` (the `plan` op); this audit does not independently authorize changes to it.

## Executive conclusion

Drew Baye's system is a credible **minimal-dose, time-efficient way to resistance train**, but the books repeatedly turn “one hard set can work” into “one failure set produces the greatest possible result.” That stronger claim does not survive the evidence. The 2026 ACSM position stand synthesized 137 reviews and more than 30,000 participants: resistance training works across many forms, hypertrophy is enhanced by higher weekly volume (about 10 or more sets per muscle per week in the aggregate evidence), and neither failure, equipment type, nor time under tension consistently determines outcomes [R1]. A 67-study dose-response meta-regression independently found positive, diminishing-return relationships between weekly volume and hypertrophy/strength [R2].

The best parts to retain are controlled technique, progressive overload, accurate logs, simple equipment, compounds for efficiency, individualized exercise selection, adequate recovery. The parts to reject or sharply qualify are single-set maximality, routine absolute failure, universal ultralow frequency, very slow cadence as uniquely beneficial, resistance-circuit/aerobic equivalence, categorical anti-running claims.

Baye's position is not static. In 2012–2015 he says failure is useful but “not absolutely necessary”; in 2025 he claims one failure set gives the “greatest possible” gain. In 2019 he retracts the unilateral Kratos ladder and isochronal progression. In 2025 he retracts condensed pre-exhaustion, rest-pause, negative-only work, and routine set extension. His 2024–2025 push/pull/legs examples are explicitly tied to testosterone and other anabolic drugs and should not be generalized to a drug-free general-population program.

For the fixed policy, the evidence supports the Monday/Thursday full-body routine: two dynamic work sets on the first six exercises, one dynamic set each on the Dual 600 Lower Back, the Dual 600 Abdominals and the calf press, plus one 90-second TSC each for neck extension and neck flexion. Dynamic work uses 5–8 repetitions, a 5-second positive and 5-second negative with a smooth unpaused reversal, and two-minute rests. The phase durations use the lower boundary of Baye's 2025 recommendation but deliberately omit his additional 2–3-second endpoint holds. The exact cadence is a fixed control preference, not a demonstrated hypertrophy or injury-prevention optimum. Rest is likewise a practical standard rather than privileged biological values. TSC is an equipment workaround rather than a uniquely superior training method, and its force cannot be quantified without measuring equipment. Dedicated aerobic work remains a distinct stimulus; the current prescription is resistance training only and does not claim to supply it. Strength starts at 07:00 and has no prescribed end time. The routine is an effective sustainable dose; it does not claim that this volume maximizes every possible hypertrophy outcome.

The final 11 exercises are audited directly against Baye's own passages in [Exercise_Selection_Primary_Source_Audit.md](Exercise_Selection_Primary_Source_Audit.md). That audit governs the exercise-specific consequences.

## Method

All seven supplied PDFs were read page by page (602 PDF pages total). Text extraction was reliable; the 2012 cover is image-only and its PDF pp. 5, 53, 85, and 113 are intentionally blank. No unreadable content page was omitted. The current claims catalog selects exercise and measurement propositions and preserves their printed/PDF mappings.

Scientific judgments prioritize systematic reviews, meta-analyses, position stands, then randomized trials and mechanistic human work. Actual PubMed records/abstracts were inspected; search snippets were not used. “Supports” below means the strongest fair steelman, not proof. “Opposes” identifies the evidence that limits the claim. Population means do not guarantee an individual response, and a method can be effective without being necessary or optimal.

The retained rows below use the verdict vocabulary. Each supplies the claim and page, strongest support, strongest opposition, permitted conclusion and policy consequence, and confidence. Reference keys resolve to full titles and stable links at the end.

## Practical interpretation

Controlled technique, precise setup records, progressive resistance and dedicated aerobic work remain useful. The evidence does not establish that single-set failure, very slow cadence, a fixed exercise order or minimal rest is universally best.

The operating policy retains the eleven exercises and their prescribed counts. Dynamic progression uses actual clean repetitions on consecutive exposures; neck TSC remains a separate 30/30/30 protocol. Scientific source descriptions above are not alternative instructions to add exercises, phases or recording fields. The prescription in `src/policy/prescription.ts` is authoritative for execution.

Baye's emphasis on simple exercises, controlled execution, progression and records is compatible with the evidence.
