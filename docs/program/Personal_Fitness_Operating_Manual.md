# Personal Fitness Operating Manual

This is the detailed operating specification for the fixed America/Chicago routine. [START_HERE.md](../../START_HERE.md) is the concise card. The executable prescription is the typed value in `src/policy/prescription.ts`; BumbleDB stores personal observations, not this plan.

## Profile and boundaries

- Identity, birth date, sex, height, and measurements live only in the ignored personal database and seed.
- Confirmed equipment: HOIST CL-2403, D-200, D-300, D-400, D-600 and PTS-1000; Precor EFX; Concept2 Model D/PM3; fixed dumbbells and adjustable benches. Bring one dedicated yoga block for neck work.
- Goals: muscle and strength, cardiorespiratory fitness, body-composition support, and reliable execution.

This is training guidance, not medical clearance. Stop for sharp or escalating pain, new numbness or weakness, loss of balance, chest pressure, faintness, or unusual breathlessness. A joint that feels progressively worse during an exercise is a stop signal, not a cue to force range.

## Fixed timing

Every day:

- 06:30: wake and immediately take one 200 mg caffeine pill.
- 14:30: caffeine cutoff.
- 21:15: wind-down begins.
- 22:00: lights out.

Strength starts at 07:00 Monday and Thursday. E-bike riding starts at 07:00 Tuesday, Friday, and Saturday. Wednesday and Sunday contain no training. An absence does not move the sequence; resume on the next scheduled day.

The strength start time creates no end time or duration limit. Complete all prescribed sets, transitions, and rests without rushing or dropping work to meet a clock deadline. The e-bike session has a 60-minute duration because its prescribed 5-minute easy, 50-minute Zone 2, and 5-minute easy segments total 60 minutes.

The sleep opportunity is eight hours and thirty minutes. Wall-clock anchors remain fixed across daylight-saving transitions, while recorded absolute intervals honestly reflect the resulting seven-and-a-half- or nine-and-a-half-hour transition night. Both training modes use 07:00 to place exercise soon after waking; research does not establish a universally superior training time.

## Strength operating sequence

Begin with the first prescribed work set. There is no general cardio preparation and no separately prescribed ramp set. Dynamic work uses familiar, stable equipment and 5–8 slow controlled repetitions rather than maximal or explosive lifting. Each TSC begins with its own gradual moderate-effort phase. The available warm-muscle meta-analysis found improvement in rate-dependent force properties but not maximum force and does not establish that a separate generalized sequence would improve this workout or prevent injury.

### Identical Monday/Thursday workout

| Order | Exercise | Work prescription | Rest |
|---:|---|---|---:|
| 1 | CL-2403 leg press | 2 × 5–8 at about 2 RIR | 120 s |
| 2 | D-200 close-underhand front pulldown | 2 × 5–8 at about 2 RIR | 120 s |
| 3 | D-300 horizontal chest press | 2 × 5–8 at about 2 RIR | 120 s |
| 4 | D-200 chest-supported mid-row | 2 × 5–8 at about 2 RIR | 120 s |
| 5 | Back-supported neutral-grip DB overhead press | 2 × 5–8 at about 2 RIR | 120 s |
| 6 | D-400 seated leg curl | 2 × 5–8 at about 2 RIR | 120 s |
| 7 | D-600 lower-back extension | 1 × 5–8 at about 2 RIR | 120 s |
| 8 | D-600 abdominal flexion | 1 × 5–8 at about 2 RIR | 120 s |
| 9 | CL-2403 straight-leg calf press | 1 × 5–8 at about 2 RIR | 120 s |
| 10 | 45° bench timed static neck extension | 1 × 90 s TSC | 120 s |
| 11 | 45° bench timed static neck flexion | 1 × 90 s TSC | workout ends |

The order number is the execution slot. Perform slots 1 through 11 exactly as written, completing all prescribed sets for one exercise before moving to the next. Do not circuit, superset, or reorder around machine availability.

The ordering is fixed for specific reasons:

1. Leg press goes first because it is the largest lower-body compound movement and should be performed before smaller local work creates fatigue.
2. Pulldown, chest press, row, and overhead press alternate pull, push, pull, push. This limits one local muscle group from unnecessarily degrading the next exercise and separates the D-200 pulldown and row rather than stacking two grip-intensive pulls.
3. Leg curl follows the large compounds because it fills the remaining knee-flexion function without reducing leg-press performance.
4. Lower-back and abdominal work follow every exercise that benefits from an unfatigued trunk, and their adjacent slots use the two functions of the same D-600 without another transition.
5. Calf press follows the larger leg and trunk work because calf fatigue should not precede the leg press; returning to the CL-2403 is intentional.
6. Neck extension and flexion are last because direct neck fatigue should not precede pressing, pulling, trunk work, or machine transitions. Extension precedes flexion, matching Baye's basic machine sequence; both use the same 45° bench and yoga block.

Keeping this order unchanged also makes load, repetition, and RIR comparisons more interpretable. No listed exercise is elective, and no accessory is added.

### Dynamic repetition standard

Every repetition uses a 5-second positive/lifting phase and a 5-second negative/lowering phase. Begin the positive phase without jerking. As either endpoint approaches, decelerate into a smooth continuous reversal: there is no scheduled isometric pause, but there is also no bounce, abrupt direction change, hard lockout, or deliberate unloading between repetitions. Do not shorten range as fatigue rises or move the torso to finish a repetition. The last accepted repetition must remain the same exercise as the first.

At this cadence, 5–8 repetitions imply approximately 50–80 seconds of dynamic movement. That arithmetic is a consequence of the cadence, not a time-under-tension target and not a limit on workout duration. Count clean repetitions; do not terminate a technically sound set merely because a stopwatch reaches a particular number. Load, clean repetitions, consistent range, estimated RIR, and pain remain the useful observations.

RIR means additional clean repetitions available without changing technique. Target about 2 RIR; do not claim exact knowledge. End the set before the next repetition would require a changed path, shortened range, bounce, or body-position shift. Do not deliberately train to failure or use set-extension methods.

The fixed 120-second rest is an execution standard, not a claim that 120 seconds is biologically unique. It clears the range where short rests commonly reduce subsequent performance; the recent hypertrophy meta-analysis found a small benefit above 60 seconds and no appreciable difference beyond roughly 90 seconds. Rest for the full interval even if the workout takes longer as a result.

### Timed static contraction standard

Neck extension and neck flexion use one continuous 90-second contraction against an immovable padded obstacle. The obstacle and the body remain still.

1. Start at zero and ramp gradually to a moderate effort over the first few seconds; maintain that level through 30 seconds.
2. Ramp gradually to a near-maximal effort; maintain it through 60 seconds.
3. Ramp gradually to the hardest effort you believe you can safely maintain; continue through 90 seconds.
4. Ramp gradually back to zero instead of releasing suddenly.

Breathe continuously and keep every non-target body segment quiet. The first moderate phase is the preparation; there is no separate TSC warm-up. Reduce effort before any pain threshold. Stop for sharp or escalating pain, numbness, tingling, dizziness, headache, visual disturbance, or loss of position. Rest 120 seconds before the next exercise.

TSC effort is not RIR. Without force-measuring equipment, neither force output nor progression can be quantified honestly. BumbleDB therefore stores only actual duration and pain, never estimated pounds, invented force, or an RIR value.

### Dynamic progression

For each dynamic exercise independently:

1. Preserve setup, range, cadence, and load.
2. Add clean repetitions while targeting about 2 RIR.
3. When every prescribed set reaches 8 repetitions at that effort on two consecutive exposures, increase by one selector position or the smallest dumbbell increment.
4. Expect repetitions to fall toward 5 after the increase.
5. If the new load prevents 5 clean repetitions, return to the preceding load at the next exposure.

There are no calendar phases. One poor exposure is not a plateau; investigate only a repeated pattern under stable setup, range, sleep opportunity, and honest effort estimates.

TSC has no selector or repetition promotion rule. Preserve the obstacle, machine or bench geometry, yoga-block placement, and 30/30/30 timing. Improvement may occur even though this setup cannot measure it; do not manufacture a progression metric to make the record look denser.

## Exercise execution

### CL-2403 leg press

Set the back and seat slide so the starting knee angle is near 90 degrees while pelvis and back remain supported. Feet are around shoulder width, knees track toes, and the bottom stops before the pelvis curls. Do not rebound or hard-lock the knees.

### D-200 close-underhand front pulldown

Clip the long lat-pulldown bar onto the D-200 cable. Do not use the rope or triangle handle. Hold the long bar underhand with palms facing toward you and hands just inside shoulder width. Set the thigh restraint snugly, lean the torso back about 30 degrees, and pull toward the upper chest. No behind-neck path, hip drive, or changing torso angle during a repetition. This is Baye's defined front-grip pulldown; a pronated wide-grip pulldown is a different exercise.

### D-300 horizontal chest press

Adjust seat and back so the handles cross the mid-sternum and shoulders remain supported through a mild chest stretch. Stack wrists over forearms and keep elbows below shoulder height.

### D-200 chest-supported mid-row

Set seat and chest pad so the handles meet the lower-to-mid sternum. Allow controlled shoulder-blade reach while the chest stays on the pad. Pull without rocking, low-back extension, or a finishing shrug.

### Back-supported neutral-grip DB overhead press

Use a high bench back, planted feet, palms facing one another, and a pain-free bottom position. Keep ribs and lumbar spine from extending. This is the only free-weight work movement because no confirmed available machine supplies a true vertical press.

### D-400 seated leg curl

Align the knee with the swing-arm pivot, place the moving roller at the lower calf, and secure the thigh restraint. Begin with a slight knee bend rather than hyperextension. Keep feet neutral and do not lift the hips or bounce.

### D-600 lower-back extension

Use the footrest and pads to prevent sliding. Use a conservative, pain-free arc and reverse smoothly without chasing end-range hyperextension.

### D-600 abdominal flexion

Secure the lower body and use trunk flexion rather than pulling with the arms. Keep pelvis and pads stable. Do not use momentum.

### CL-2403 straight-leg calf press

Place the forefoot securely on the footplate, keep knees softly extended and still, and move only at the ankle. Establish a comfortable range gradually, control the turnaround, and do not bounce.

### 45° bench timed static neck extension

Set the adjustable bench to 45 degrees and use the same dedicated yoga block every session. Lie face-up with the block centered under the back of the head rather than the cervical spine. Establish a neutral neck: do not begin with the chin forced toward the chest or the head dropped backward. Press the head backward into the block using the 30/30/30 TSC protocol without allowing the head, torso, or block to move.

### 45° bench timed static neck flexion

Keep the bench at 45 degrees and use the same block orientation. Lie face-down, brace through the arms so body weight is supported independently of the head, and center the forehead on the block. Establish neutral alignment and press the forehead into the block using the 30/30/30 protocol without nodding or moving the torso. Do not turn this into a neck bridge.

Extension and flexion are the complete neck prescription. Lateral flexion and rotation are deliberately excluded: Baye's 2019 update says extension and flexion cover nearly all the same musculature, while the existing overhead press already trains the shoulder-elevation contribution. A harness and manual resistance are also rejected because the 45° padded bench is more braced and reproducible with the confirmed equipment.

## Geometry and initial resistance calibration

Height cannot determine a pin or adjustment hole. Begin near the middle of an adjustment range, then move one mark at a time until the machine path and relevant joints align. Record actual seat, back, thigh, chest, footrest, range, or slide marks as personal effective-dated setup observations.

When establishing a new selector exercise:

1. Fix the geometry first.
2. At position 1, perform 3 clean repetitions using the prescribed 5-second positive and 5-second negative.
3. If the probe is plainly easy and at least 5 additional clean repetitions clearly remain, rest 90 seconds and perform another 3-repetition probe one position heavier.
4. Continue while every test remains easy and identical. Stop before grinding.
5. Rest 2 minutes and choose a conservative tested position expected to yield 5–8 clean repetitions near the target effort.

This is one-time calibration, not a recurring preliminary sequence. D-series selectors are ordinal positions because cams, lever arms, cables, and an unknown top-plate mass prevent a valid output-pound conversion. The CL-2403 factory chart is the sole exception: pin 1 is 47 lb and each step adds 23 lb of stated actual resistance.

The TSC movements do not use this load ladder. For neck extension and flexion, record the observed 45° bench mark and yoga-block placement as personal setup facts.

## E-bike operating sequence

At 07:00 on Tuesday, Friday, and Saturday:

1. Ride 5 minutes easy.
2. Accumulate 50 minutes in WHOOP Zone 2.
3. Ride 5 minutes easy.

The target is zone time. Hills, wind, battery assist, route, and speed may change; the interval remains 60 minutes. Stop at 08:00 regardless of a derived WHOOP score.

## Sleep and caffeine

The eight-hour-thirty-minute opportunity remains fixed every night. Morning light, the 06:30 wake, and the 21:15 wind-down are the anchors. The 200 mg pill is the fixed policy dose, not a reason to escalate. The 14:30 cutoff leaves seven and a half hours before lights out.

Set WHOOP as follows:

1. Open **Tonight's Sleep** and select **Improve My Sleep** as the Sleep Planner goal.
2. Set **Wake-Up Time** to **06:30**.
3. Turn the Wake Alarm on and select **Exact Time**.
4. Set the recurring wake-up schedule to every day of the week.

Do not select **Sleep Goal** or **In the Green** as the alarm mode; both can wake you inside a variable one-hour window using WHOOP-derived targets. WHOOP does not provide a fixed-bedtime alarm in this flow. Its suggested bedtime is informational only: the actual schedule remains 21:15 wind-down and 22:00 lights out.

WHOOP sleep need, debt, efficiency, stages, performance, recovery, and strain cannot change bedtime, wake time, exercise selection, volume, or ride duration. Persistent insomnia, excessive daytime sleepiness, loud snoring/gasping, palpitations, or another concerning symptom warrants clinical evaluation rather than app optimization.

## Data boundary and review

The TypeScript prescription describes what should happen. BumbleDB contains only personal data:

- profile;
- observed strength/e-bike activities and concrete dynamic or TSC work sets;
- observed effective setup marks;
- sleep intervals, body weight, and waist;
- WHOOP UUIDs, exact intervals/offsets, average/max HR, six integer zone durations, and nap classification.

It contains no machine catalog, recurring schedule, workout template, caffeine rule, e-bike recipe, research provenance, completed/missed state, or proprietary WHOOP score. Partial sessions contain only work that occurred.

Review facts, not vendor projections: load/repetition trends, technique-stable effort estimates, pain, Zone 2 duration, sleep intervals, and setup changes.
