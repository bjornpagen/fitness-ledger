# Exercise Selection: Drew Baye Primary-Source Audit

## Conclusion

The fixed 11-exercise workout implements Baye's basic machine template, adds stable knee flexion, and includes his direct neck extension/flexion recommendation. It deliberately omits the equipment-workaround dorsiflexion movement because the added setup complexity does not justify its marginal value.

In *High Intensity Workouts*, Baye says a minimum routine should include a squatting movement, vertical pull, vertical push, horizontal pull, horizontal push and trunk extension, then add direct abdominal, calf and neck work (PDF pp. 16–17; printed pp. 12–13). His example machine workout ends with calf raise, neck extension and neck flexion. The 2019 update supplies safe padded TSC dorsiflexion and says neck extension/flexion make lateral work unnecessary (PDF pp. 24–25). The 2025 supplement says his programs include the big six plus direct neck, forearms and/or grip, calves and shins, then identifies a leg-extension ankle pad and a 45° bench with yoga block as the practical equipment-limited implementations (PDF pp. 16–20; printed pp. 13–17).

This is a source-concordance audit, not an endorsement of Baye's one-set/failure/frequency doctrine. Dynamic sets, RIR, rest and cadence follow the broader evidence audit. TSC is used only where the gym lacks a dedicated dynamic machine; it is recorded without fictitious load, force, or RIR.

## Full-spectrum check

| Region/function | Direct exercise or compound coverage |
|---|---|
| Quads and hip extensors | Leg press |
| Hamstrings/knee flexors | Seated leg curl; leg press and trunk extension contribute |
| Calves/plantarflexors | Straight-leg calf press |
| Trunk extensors | D-600 lower-back extension |
| Abdominals/obliques | D-600 abdominal flexion; compounds add bracing |
| Lats, teres, biceps and forearms | Pulldown plus row |
| Mid/rear shoulder, rhomboids and middle/lower traps | Chest-supported row plus pulldown |
| Chest, triceps and anterior shoulder | Chest press |
| Deltoids, triceps and upper chest | Overhead press |
| Neck extensors and flexors | 45° bench TSC neck extension and flexion |

The routine covers upper and lower body, all six basic movement functions, plantarflexion at the ankle, and direct neck flexion/extension. It does not directly isolate every anatomical subdivision. Baye himself says a huge variety is unnecessary and warns against multiplying curls, grips, and angles (PDF pp. 17–18; printed pp. 13–14).

Direct wrist flexion/extension and grip, hip flexion, hip adduction/abduction, and oblique flexion appear in Baye's expanded programs. They are not hard omissions here. Pulling already gives substantial forearm/grip work, leg press trains the major hip extensors and adductors, compounds require frontal-plane stabilization, and abdominal flexion plus bracing trains the trunk. Baye distributes these supplementary movements across rotating programs rather than requiring all of them in one fixed session. Adding them all would undermine the fixed one-hour, no-rotation policy for increasingly specialized returns.

## Hard deletions and why they stay deleted

- DB stiff-leg/Romanian deadlift: the D-400 curl plus D-600 extension cover the intended hamstring/trunk work with less balance and hinge-skill demand.
- DB bench press: duplicates the D-300 horizontal press.
- Lateral raise and shrug: overhead pressing, rowing and pulldown already train the shoulder girdle; Baye's own text rejects unnecessary variation.
- Curl: pulldown and row already train elbow flexors, and the goal is not maximal arm specialization.
- Standing DB calf raise: Baye gives the leg-press substitution explicitly.
- Floor abdominal drills and static holds: D-600 flexion supplies direct, loadable abdominal work on confirmed equipment.
- Leg extension: the leg press already supplies the knee-dominant function; the leg curl adds the missing knee-flexion function without redundant quadriceps isolation.
- Smith squat: duplicates the leg press and adds setup/coordination cost without filling a missing pattern.

## Exercise order

Baye recommends large compound work first, alternating pulling with non-pulling work to reduce grip limitation, and placing direct low-back/abdominal work after exercises needing trunk stability (PDF pp. 50–51; printed pp. 46–47). The current order follows that logic: large leg work first; pull, push, pull, push; leg curl; small anterior-leg work while already at the D-400; then lower back, abdomen and calf; neck last. Separating the D-200 pulldown and row prevents two grip-intensive pulls from being consecutive. Trunk work is delayed until it cannot degrade earlier positioning, the two D-600 functions remain adjacent, and direct neck fatigue cannot affect any compound movement. The numbered slot is the mandatory execution order, and it stays fixed so setup and performance remain comparable.
